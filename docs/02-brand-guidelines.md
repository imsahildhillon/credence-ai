# Credence AI — Brand Guidelines

**Product:** Credence AI — Evidence-Based Talent Intelligence Platform
**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** July 2026
**Owner:** Design & Brand
**Companion document:** [01-product-definition-document.md](01-product-definition-document.md)

---

## How to use this handbook

This is the official design and brand reference for everyone who ships anything with the Credence name on it — product, marketing, sales decks, support replies, social posts. It is a decision-making tool, not a mood board. Every rule here traces back to one strategic fact from the PDD:

> **Credence sells trust.** Our product is verified interpretation of evidence. Therefore the brand itself must *behave like evidence*: precise, calm, transparent, and never overclaiming.

When a design or copy decision isn't covered here, ask: *"Would a skeptical recruiter trust us more or less after seeing this?"* That question resolves most disputes.

---

## 1. Brand Mission

**Make proof of skill visible.**

Credence exists to replace hiring's weak proxies — brand names, keywords, CGPA — with demonstrated capability. The brand's job is to make that replacement feel *safe*: safe for a recruiter to defend to their hiring manager, and safe for a student to stake their career on.

Every brand expression should advance one of three feelings:

1. **Credibility** — "This platform's judgment can be trusted."
2. **Clarity** — "I understand exactly what this assessment means and why."
3. **Momentum** — "I can get better, and I can see how." (Students are on a trajectory, never a verdict.)

---

## 2. Brand Personality

Credence is **the trusted technical assessor who's on your side** — think of a brilliant, fair-minded staff engineer who reviews your work rigorously, shows you exactly why, and genuinely wants you to grow.

Five personality traits, each with a boundary so they don't drift:

| Trait | What it looks like | The drift we prevent |
|---|---|---|
| **Rigorous** | Precise language, evidence citations, confidence levels | Not cold, bureaucratic, or academic |
| **Candid** | Says the true thing plainly, including limits of our own assessments | Not blunt, harsh, or judgmental |
| **Encouraging** | Frames every gap as a next step; celebrates real progress | Not cheerleading, hype, or empty positivity |
| **Calm** | Unhurried layouts, measured motion, no urgency theatrics | Not boring, sterile, or lifeless |
| **Modern-technical** | Fluent in developers' world (code, repos, systems) | Not gatekeeping jargon or hacker-aesthetic clichés |

**Personality litmus test:** If a piece of copy or design could belong to a lottery app, a hustle-culture influencer, or an enterprise compliance tool, it is off-brand. We sit between those poles: serious about evidence, human about people.

---

## 3. Core Values

Derived directly from the PDD's product principles; these are the brand's non-negotiables.

1. **Evidence over claims.** We never assert what we can't show. In the product, every number links to its proof. In marketing, every stat has a source. The brand never uses vague superlatives ("revolutionary," "world's best") — those are exactly the unverifiable claims we exist to eliminate.
2. **Both sides, one standard.** Students are users, not inventory. Brand materials never talk about candidates as "supply," "pipeline units," or things to be "filtered." Recruiters and students are addressed with equal respect and equal honesty.
3. **Explainable, contestable, humble.** Every assessment can be understood by a non-expert and challenged by its subject. The brand voice admits uncertainty rather than faking precision.
4. **Growth over judgment.** Nobody is a score. The brand always pairs assessment with direction. We measure trajectories.
5. **Fairness as identity.** Correcting proxy bias is our founding premise. Imagery, language, examples, and defaults must reflect the full diversity of who builds software — non-elite colleges, self-taught paths, all genders and geographies.

## 4. Tone of Voice

**Core voice:** *A precise, warm expert.* Confident because we have evidence; warm because careers are personal.

### Voice attributes

- **Plain before clever.** Short sentences. Concrete nouns. No metaphor where a literal statement works.
- **Specific before general.** "Your test coverage is the gap for backend roles" beats "improve your skills."
- **Active and direct.** "We analyzed 12 repositories" — not "12 repositories were analyzed."
- **Honest about confidence.** "High confidence, based on 14 months of commit history" — we say *how sure* we are, always.

### Tone shifts by context (voice stays, tone flexes)

| Context | Tone | Example register |
|---|---|---|
| Student receives strong assessment | Warm, specific, momentum-building | "Your API design work is genuinely strong — here's what makes it stand out to recruiters." |
| Student receives weak assessment | Respectful, concrete, forward-looking. Never euphemistic, never brutal. | "Your projects show solid fundamentals. The gap for backend roles is testing depth — here's the highest-impact fix." |
| Recruiter-facing analysis | Neutral, evidentiary, decision-ready | "Strong originality signals: 3 independent projects, one deployed with active users. Interview reasoning: above benchmark." |
| Errors & failures | Calm, accountable, actionable | "We couldn't analyze this repository. Usually this means it's private — here's how to grant access." |
| Marketing | Confident, understated, evidence-led | "Recruiters using Credence doubled interview pass rates. Here's the data." |
| Legal/consent moments | Maximum clarity, zero persuasion | Consent screens inform; they never nudge. |

### Never

- Hype vocabulary: *revolutionary, game-changing, 10x, crushing it, unlock your potential.*
- Fear vocabulary: *don't get left behind, beat the competition, stand out or lose out.*
- Judgment vocabulary about people: *weak candidate, poor performer, failed.* (Assessments describe **evidence and skills**, never worth.)
- False intimacy: *Hey superstar! We missed you!*

## 5. Messaging Principles

1. **Lead with the mechanism, not the promise.** Our differentiation is *how* we know. "AI interviews grounded in your own code" is more persuasive than "verified skills." Show the mechanism and the promise becomes believable.
2. **One audience per message.** Student messaging and recruiter messaging are never blended in one asset. The value propositions are complementary but distinct; mixing them muddies both.
3. **Evidence is the hero.** In every screenshot, demo, and case study, the star is the *evidence trail* — the repo link, the transcript excerpt, the confidence level. That's the category claim made visible.
4. **Name the enemy carefully.** Our enemy is *the broken proxy system* — never recruiters (our customers), never other candidates, never specific competitors by name in brand materials.
5. **Claims carry receipts.** Any quantitative claim in public materials must cite its basis. If we can't cite it, we don't ship it. (This is a brand rule, not just a legal one — it *is* the brand.)
6. **The strong-candidate message matters.** Per the PDD's adverse-selection risk: messaging must always include why *top* candidates join (differentiation, interview-skip, benchmarking). Credence is never positioned as remedial.

**Message hierarchy (canonical):**
- **Category line:** The trust layer for technical hiring.
- **Student promise:** Prove what you can actually do — and learn exactly how to become more hireable.
- **Recruiter promise:** Screen on evidence, not claims.
- **Mechanism proof point:** Assessments grounded in real work — actual code, actual projects, interviews about *your own* repositories.

## 6. Logo Philosophy

The Credence mark should embody **verification with humanity** — the moment evidence becomes trust.

**Conceptual direction (for the identity designer):**
- The mark should evoke *attestation*: a seal, a checkmark evolved beyond cliché, a signature, or the geometric idea of one form validating another (e.g., two shapes interlocking with precision).
- It must feel at home in two worlds simultaneously: a developer's terminal and a recruiter's boardroom deck. Geometric, balanced, engineered — but with one humanist gesture (a curve, an asymmetry) so it never reads as a compliance stamp.
- Avoid: shield clichés (security-industry), magnifying glasses (surveillance connotation — we are consent-based), upward-arrow/rocket clichés (hustle-culture), brain/circuit clichés (generic AI).

**System rules:**
- One primary lockup (mark + wordmark), one standalone mark for avatars/favicons, one monochrome version. No other variants.
- The wordmark is always set in the brand's primary typeface family — "Credence" with a capital C, "AI" only in formal/legal contexts; the product is called **Credence** in running text.
- Clear space: minimum one x-height of the wordmark on all sides. The mark never sits on photography without a scrim, never gets gradients, shadows, or outlines added.
- The logo never animates in product UI except at first-load, once, subtly.

## 7. Color System (with Semantic Roles)

### Philosophy

Color at Credence does one job above all others: **encode meaning consistently**. Because our product renders judgments about people, color misuse is not a cosmetic bug — it's a fairness bug. A student must never feel "graded in red," and a recruiter must never misread decoration as data.

Two rules govern everything:
1. **Semantic colors are reserved.** The colors that mean "strength," "gap," "confidence," or "warning" appear *only* when carrying that meaning — never as decoration.
2. **Neutral is the default.** Most of every screen is neutral. Color is spent sparingly, so that when it appears, it *is* information.

### Palette structure

**Primary — "Credence Blue" (deep, saturated blue-indigo family)**
The brand color. Blue-indigo for its associations with trust, depth, and calm authority — deliberately deeper and more saturated than generic corporate blue, so it feels engineered rather than bureaucratic.
- *Roles:* brand moments, primary actions, links, focus states, selected states.
- *Rule:* one primary action per view carries the primary color. If everything is blue, nothing is.

**Neutrals — warm gray scale (10–12 steps)**
Warm-leaning grays (never blue-tinted steel, which reads cold and institutional). These carry 90% of every interface: text, surfaces, borders, backgrounds. Both a light theme and a dark theme are first-class; the dark theme serves developer contexts (students reviewing code evidence) and must be designed, not inverted.

**Semantic accents (reserved — meaning-carrying only):**

| Role | Hue family | Used exclusively for | Never used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strength / verified** | Green (deep, calm — not neon "success" green) | Verified evidence, strong skill signals, completed verification | Generic success toasts, decoration, marketing accents |
| **Growth / opportunity** | Amber-gold (warm, optimistic) | Skill gaps framed as opportunities, improvement recommendations, in-progress states | Warnings or errors — growth is *positive* in our system, and its color must never share a hue with alarm |
| **Alert / integrity** | Red-coral (used rarely, softly) | Destructive actions, integrity flags, genuine errors | Low scores or skill gaps — **a person's assessment is never rendered in red** |
| **Confidence / AI** | Violet (adjacent to primary) | AI-generated content markers, confidence indicators, explainability affordances | General branding — violet signals "the AI is speaking; here's its certainty" |

**Data-visualization palette:** a distinct, accessible categorical set (5–6 hues at matched lightness) reserved for charts, plus a single-hue sequential ramp (built on the primary) for intensity scales. Skill-level scales use **sequential neutrals-to-blue**, never red-to-green: capability is a spectrum of development, not a traffic light.

### Hard rules
- Every color pairing ships only after passing contrast standards (see §13).
- Color is never the sole carrier of meaning — always paired with a label, icon, or pattern.
- No pure black (#000) on pure white; text uses near-black neutrals to reduce harshness.

## 8. Typography

### Philosophy
Typography is where "precise, warm expert" becomes visible. We pair a **humanist grotesque sans** (warm, highly legible, modern) for interface and headings with a **monospace** for evidence.

**Primary typeface — humanist grotesque sans** (e.g., in the spirit of Inter/Söhne class faces; final selection by identity designer, must have: tall x-height, open apertures, tabular figures, full weight range, excellent rendering at UI sizes, broad script support for global expansion).
- Carries all UI text, headings, marketing.

**Evidence typeface — monospace companion**
- Reserved *exclusively* for evidence artifacts: code excerpts, repository names, commit references, interview transcript quotes, raw data values.
- This reservation is a brand signature: **when you see monospace, you are looking at primary evidence, untouched by us.** Interpretation is set in the sans; evidence is set in mono. The typographic boundary *is* the explainability boundary.

### Type system rules
- A modular scale with a small number of fixed sizes (roughly 6 in-product); no arbitrary sizes.
- Body text is comfortable and unheroic: generous line-height (~1.5–1.6), 60–75 character measure for reading contexts.
- Weight discipline: regular for body, medium for emphasis and labels, semibold for headings. Bold is rare; ALL-CAPS is reserved for small labels/overlines with letter-spacing, never for headlines (caps read as shouting; we don't shout).
- Numbers in assessments always use tabular figures so scores and metrics align in scans.
- No italics for emphasis in UI (poor legibility at small sizes); use weight or color.
- Marketing may scale the same system up — it may not introduce new typefaces. Decks, site, and product must be recognizably one family.

---

## 9. Iconography

- **Style:** stroke-based (outline) icons on a consistent grid, uniform stroke weight, rounded joins — geometric structure with softened corners, echoing the personality (rigorous + warm). One consistent visual weight across the whole set; no mixing filled and outlined styles in one context.
- **Filled variants** exist only as *selected/active states* of their outline twin.
- **Semantic icons are reserved like semantic colors.** The verified-check, the confidence indicator, the AI-marker, and the integrity-flag icons are sacred: fixed shapes, fixed colors, never used decoratively, never redrawn per-surface. A recruiter should be able to trust the verified-check the way one trusts a seatbelt icon.
- **Metaphor guidance:** prefer evidence metaphors (document, link, graph node, commit dot) over judgment metaphors (gavel, trophy, ranking podium) and surveillance metaphors (eye, magnifier-on-person, crosshair). We assess work, not people; icons must not imply watching or ranking humans.
- Icons never appear without accessible labels in product (tooltip minimum, visible label preferred).

## 10. Layout Principles

1. **Calm density.** Our users make consequential decisions; layouts must support sustained reading, not dopamine scanning. Generous whitespace, one clear focal hierarchy per screen, no competing panels shouting for attention.
2. **Evidence adjacency.** The defining layout pattern of Credence: **no claim without its evidence within one interaction.** Every score, badge, or statement is laid out with its proof beside it, beneath it, or one click away — never orphaned. If a layout separates a claim from its evidence trail, the layout is wrong.
3. **Progressive disclosure, honest depth.** Summaries first, drill-down always available, and the drill-down path is *visible* (a recruiter should always sense "there is more underneath, and I can reach it"). Depth is never hidden to make the product feel simpler than the decision it supports.
4. **The grid is quiet.** A conventional column grid, consistent spacing scale (one spacing system product-wide), aligned edges. Novel layouts are spent only on the moments that define us — the credibility report, the evidence trail — never on settings pages.
5. **Two-audience symmetry.** Student-facing and recruiter-facing surfaces share the same grid, spacing, and component language. The platform must feel like *one fair system viewed from two sides* — if the recruiter side looks premium and the student side looks free-tier, we've broken value #2.
6. **Reading order = reasoning order.** Layouts present information in the order a careful evaluator would reason: context → evidence → interpretation → confidence → action. Not conclusion-first sensationalism.

## 11. Component Philosophy

- **One system, no forks.** A single component library serves student, recruiter, and marketing surfaces. Divergence requires a written exception from design leadership.
- **Components encode policy.** Our most important components are *governance made tangible*:
  - The **Evidence Card** (claim + source + confidence, inseparable) — you cannot render a claim without wiring its evidence; the component's API refuses.
  - The **Confidence Indicator** — a standard element that appears wherever the AI asserts; it cannot be omitted or restyled.
  - The **AI-Content Marker** — anything AI-generated is born labeled (see §16).
  - The **Consent Surface** — visibility and sharing controls follow one fixed, maximally-clear pattern everywhere they occur.
- **Boring by default, opinionated where it counts.** Buttons, forms, tables, and navigation follow platform conventions users already know. Our design originality budget is spent entirely on the evidence-and-explainability layer — the components no one else has.
- **States are designed, not defaulted.** Every component ships with designed empty, loading, error, and low-confidence states. The empty state of a student profile is a motivational onboarding moment; the loading state of an analysis explains *what is being analyzed*; neither is an afterthought spinner.
- **Accessibility is a component property** — a component isn't "done" until it passes §13; there is no "accessible version" of a component, only the component.

## 12. Motion Guidelines

**Philosophy: motion is explanation, not decoration.** In a product that renders judgments, gratuitous animation reads as manipulation. Motion earns its place only by doing one of four jobs:

1. **Continuity** — showing where something came from or went (a drill-down expanding from its summary; evidence unfolding beneath its claim).
2. **Causality** — confirming that an action produced an effect (a consent toggle visibly taking hold).
3. **Progress honesty** — long-running AI analysis shows *staged, truthful* progress ("Analyzing repository 3 of 7") rather than indeterminate spinners or fake progress bars.
4. **Focus** — one restrained entrance for the single most important element of a defining moment (the credibility report reveal).

**Rules:**
- Durations short and calm: ~150–250ms for micro-interactions, ~300–400ms for structural transitions. Standard ease-out for entrances, ease-in for exits. Nothing bounces, nothing overshoots — springy physics reads playful; we are calm.
- **Assessment results never animate theatrically.** No count-up scores, no confetti, no dramatic reveals of numbers that affect someone's career. Results appear promptly and settle quietly. (Celebration is allowed for *student progress milestones* — completing an interview, closing a gap — and even then: brief, warm, dignified.)
- All motion respects reduced-motion preferences with full functional equivalence.
- Marketing motion may be more expressive but inherits the same physics — the website and the product must feel like the same brand in motion.

## 13. Accessibility Standards

Accessibility is a founding-premise obligation: a platform whose mission is fair evaluation cannot itself exclude users.

**Baseline: WCAG 2.2 AA is the ship gate for everything.** AAA is the target for the two highest-stakes reading surfaces: assessment results and consent screens.

**Specific commitments:**
- Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (normal) / 3:1 (large); UI components and meaningful graphics ≥ 3:1. Both themes independently audited.
- Full keyboard operability with visible focus states (focus styling uses the primary color and is never suppressed).
- Screen-reader-first semantics for the evidence trail: a claim, its evidence link, and its confidence level must be announced *together*, in reasoning order — the explainability promise holds non-visually.
- Color never sole meaning-carrier (per §7); charts ship with patterned/labeled alternatives and data tables.
- Motion: reduced-motion honored (per §12); no flashing content, ever.
- Language: assessment explanations target plain-language readability (educated general reader, not just engineers) — a recruiter without a CS degree is a first-class reader of every explanation.
- Touch targets ≥ 44px; forms fully labeled with errors announced and described in text.
- Timed experiences (AI interviews) provide accommodations: extensions, pauses, and an explicit accessibility-needs pathway — being assessed *fairly* includes being assessed accessibly.

Accessibility review is part of design review, not a later QA pass. Violations at ship-gate are release blockers, equal in severity to data bugs.

## 14. Illustration Style

**Role:** illustrations humanize the abstract (trust, evidence, growth) in marketing, onboarding, and empty states. They never appear inside assessment or evidence surfaces — no decoration where judgment lives.

**Style direction:**
- **Constructed, not sketchy.** Geometric compositions built from the brand's shape language (the interlocking/attestation motif from the logo), with precise lines and considered color — engineered warmth, matching the personality. Avoid the interchangeable "corporate flat people" style (oversized limbs, generic poses) that signals template startup.
- **Abstract-first for concepts, human-second for people.** Systems, evidence, and skill graphs are depicted abstractly (nodes, layers, connecting forms). When people appear, they are specific and dignified: real diversity of gender, ethnicity, body type, and setting — including non-elite, non-Silicon-Valley contexts, reflecting our actual users (value #5). People are shown *doing* work (building, reviewing, discussing), never being inspected, ranked on podiums, or filtered through funnels.
- **Palette:** neutrals + primary blue as the base, with one semantic accent maximum per illustration, used meaningfully.
- **Metaphor bans:** no rockets/hockey sticks (hype), no magnifying-glass-over-person or eyes (surveillance), no podiums/trophies/leaderboards (ranking humans), no robots-replacing-humans imagery (our AI assists judgment; it doesn't replace people).

## 15. Dashboard Design Principles

The credibility report (student) and candidate search/profile views (recruiter) are the brand's defining surfaces. Principles:

1. **Decision-first hierarchy.** Each dashboard opens with the one question its user came to answer — student: *"Where do I stand, and what do I do next?"*; recruiter: *"Who should I look at, and why?"* Everything else is subordinate.
2. **Every number carries three companions:** its evidence link, its confidence level, and its plain-language meaning. A bare metric is a defect (per §10's evidence adjacency and §16).
3. **Trajectory over snapshot for students.** Student dashboards foreground *change* — progress since last month, gap-closing momentum — before absolute standing. The recruiter view may lead with current standing; the student view leads with direction. Same data, audience-honest framings.
4. **Comparison without humiliation.** Students are benchmarked against *role requirements* ("readiness for Backend Engineer"), never against a visible ranking of peers. Recruiters compare candidates side-by-side; students are never shown their own leaderboard position (per PDD exclusion of leaderboards).
5. **Restraint in data-ink.** Few charts, each earning its place; no gauges, no 3D, no decorative sparklines. Prefer the chart form that makes the *decision* easiest, not the one that looks most impressive in a demo.
6. **Empty and low-data states teach.** A new student's sparse dashboard explains exactly which evidence would light up which sections — the empty state *is* the improvement engine's first recommendation.
7. **Exportable honesty.** Anything a recruiter can screenshot into a hiring packet must carry its confidence and evidence context *within the exported frame* — our claims travel with their receipts.

## 16. AI Explainability Guidelines

This is the most brand-critical section: explainability *is* the product promise rendered in design.

1. **AI content is always born labeled.** Every AI-generated assessment, summary, or recommendation carries the AI-Content Marker (violet accent, fixed icon, "AI assessment" label). Users never wonder whether a human or the system wrote something.
2. **The three-layer explanation pattern.** Every assessment renders in three layers, always in this order:
   - **The finding** (plain language, one sentence): "Strong backend fundamentals with a testing gap."
   - **The evidence** (monospace, linked, inspectable): the specific repos, commits, transcript excerpts that produced the finding.
   - **The reasoning** (how the evidence led to the finding, and what would change it): "Based on 3 independent projects over 14 months. This assessment would strengthen with test coverage in a deployed project."
3. **Confidence is always visible and always honest.** Standard vocabulary — *High / Moderate / Preliminary* confidence — each defined in plain language on hover/tap, each tied to its basis ("Preliminary: based on limited evidence — 1 project, no interview yet"). We never render precision the model doesn't have: no "87.3% skill score" theater.
4. **Contestability is a visible affordance.** "Question this assessment" appears on every assessment surface, styled as a legitimate action, not buried in a menu. The dispute path is a feature, not a complaint form.
5. **Limits are stated in-line.** Where our analysis cannot see (private repos not shared, skills with no evidence yet), the UI says so explicitly rather than silently omitting — absence of evidence is displayed as *"not yet assessed,"* never as a low score.
6. **The student sees what the recruiter sees.** No hidden recruiter-only judgments about a person. Asymmetric visibility of assessments would poison trust on both sides; transparency symmetry is enforced at the design level.
7. **Language rules for AI claims:** assessments describe *evidence and skills*, never character ("the code shows limited test coverage," never "the candidate is careless"); verbs of observation over verbs of verdict ("we found / the evidence shows," not "you failed to").

## 17. UX Writing Examples

Canonical before/after pairs; use these as calibration for all product copy.

**Onboarding (student)**
- ✗ "Unlock your dream job with AI-powered insights!"
- ✓ "Connect your GitHub. We'll analyze what you've actually built and show you how recruiters will see it."

**Strong assessment (student)**
- ✗ "Congratulations! You're a rockstar developer! 🎉"
- ✓ "Your API design is a genuine strength — consistent across all three projects, with high confidence. Recruiters screening for backend roles will notice this."

**Weak-signal assessment (student)**
- ✗ "Your testing skills are poor." / ✗ "Testing: 34/100."
- ✓ "Testing is your biggest gap for backend roles. None of your projects include automated tests yet — adding a test suite to your deployed project is the single highest-impact improvement available to you."

**Low confidence (both audiences)**
- ✗ "Skill verified ✓" (on thin evidence)
- ✓ "Preliminary assessment — based on one project. Completing an AI interview would give this a firmer basis."

**Recruiter candidate summary**
- ✗ "Top 5% candidate! Don't miss out!"
- ✓ "Strong originality signals: 3 independent projects, one deployed with active users. Interview reasoning above benchmark for early-career backend. Gap noted: no collaborative-repo experience."

**Consent moment (student)**
- ✗ "Boost your visibility! Recruiters are waiting — go public now!"
- ✓ "Make your profile visible to recruiters? You control this. You can see who viewed your profile and turn visibility off at any time."

**Contact request (student notification)**
- ✓ "A recruiter at a 120-person SaaS company would like to contact you about a backend role. They've seen your public profile. Accept, decline, or view the role first — your choice, no penalty for declining."

**Error state**
- ✗ "Oops! Something went wrong 😅"
- ✓ "We couldn't finish analyzing this repository. Usually this means it's private or was moved. Re-check access, or skip it — your other 4 repositories are unaffected."

**Analysis in progress**
- ✗ [indeterminate spinner]
- ✓ "Analyzing repository 3 of 7: looking at code structure and commit history. About 2 minutes remaining."

**Integrity flag (recruiter view — handled with care)**
- ✗ "⚠ CHEATING DETECTED"
- ✓ "Integrity note: this project closely matches a public tutorial. It's excluded from originality scoring. The candidate's other two projects show independent work."

## 18. Brand Do's and Don'ts

### Do
- **Do** show the evidence trail in every demo, screenshot, and case study — it's our category claim made visible.
- **Do** state confidence levels everywhere the AI speaks, in product and in marketing.
- **Do** address students and recruiters with equal respect and identical honesty.
- **Do** use monospace exclusively for raw evidence — protect the typographic boundary.
- **Do** spend color sparingly and semantically; let neutral surfaces carry the interface.
- **Do** frame every gap as a next step; pair every assessment with direction.
- **Do** show real diversity of backgrounds — non-elite colleges and self-taught paths are our founding constituency, not an afterthought.
- **Do** cite a source for every public stat. No receipt, no claim.
- **Do** keep motion calm, brief, and explanatory.

### Don't
- **Don't** render any assessment of a person in red, ever.
- **Don't** use hype ("revolutionary," "10x"), fear ("don't get left behind"), or hustle-culture language — we are the antidote to unverifiable claims.
- **Don't** show leaderboards, podiums, trophies, or any imagery ranking humans.
- **Don't** use surveillance metaphors (eyes, magnifying glasses over people, crosshairs) — we are consent-based analysis, not monitoring.
- **Don't** animate scores, add confetti to results, or count numbers up dramatically — careers aren't slot machines.
- **Don't** fake precision: no decimal-point scores, no "87.3% match," no certainty theater.
- **Don't** refer to students as pipeline, supply, inventory, or leads — in any internal or external material.
- **Don't** blend student and recruiter messaging in one asset.
- **Don't** create off-system components, one-off colors, or new typefaces for a campaign. The system is the brand.
- **Don't** ship any surface where a claim appears without its evidence within one interaction — this is the one rule that, if broken, means the product is no longer Credence.
